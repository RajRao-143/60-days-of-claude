How targets are calculated

  • Energy: basal needs from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, multiplied by an activity factor (1.2 to 1.9). Weight loss uses 85% of that figure, weight gain 110%, with a floor of 1,500 kcal for men and 1,200 kcal for women.
  • Protein: 0.85 to 1.5 g per kg of body weight depending on activity, with small increases for age 60 and over or a weight goal, kept within 10 to 30% of energy.
  • Fat and carbohydrate: fat is 27% of energy. Carbohydrate fills the remainder.
  • Fibre: 14 g per 1,000 kcal, at least 25 g.
  • Micronutrients: daily reference values adapted from ICMR-NIN 2020 for Indian adults, adjusted for gender and age band. Sodium is treated as a limit, not a goal (2,000 mg).
  • Status labels: under 50% of target is deficient, 50 to 79% is low, 80% and above is met, and intake far above a target is flagged as excess.
  • Averages: the 2-day view averages only the days that have food logged.

Food values are rounded approximations for typical home-cooked or ready-to-eat portions. Cooked dishes include normal cooking salt. Real foods vary with variety, recipe and preparation.

Educational disclaimer

NutriScope is an educational tool. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition and does not replace advice from a doctor or registered dietitian. Targets are general reference values for healthy adults; they do not cover pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, older adults with frailty, or people with medical conditions such as kidney disease, diabetes or anaemia. Two days of food records cannot show your long-term intake, and self-reported portions are often inaccurate. Risk labels describe patterns in the food you logged, not your health status. Do not start supplements or stop medication based on this app.

NutriScope runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere; it is saved only on this device. Educational use only, not medical advice.
