## 5. Go-To-Market Strategy

| Decision | Recommendation | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Initial customer segment (one) | Final-year undergraduate students at Indian colleges, including non-CS students | **[ASSUMPTION]** |
| Main distribution channel (one) | Campus cyber-awareness sessions with cyber clubs or IT cells | **[ASSUMPTION]** |

**GTM flow (one path):** Cyber club or IT cell agrees to host → free 30–45 minute session → attendees open the check-up → three sourced actions → 7-day follow-up → day-14 return prompt → fake-door Pro button and a pilot conversation with the host institution.

**Verified hooks for this channel**

- UGC asked colleges, universities, and other higher-education institutions to observe Cyber Jagrookta Diwas monthly; the suggested slot was the first Wednesday, 11am, one hour, online (notice of 18 Nov 2021). **[FACT]** Current practice is **[UNKNOWN]**.
- UGC requested institutions to implement its cyber security syllabus at UG and PG level (launched 6 Oct 2022); the article describes a request, not a mandate. **[FACT]**
- Delhi University and I4C signed an MoU covering cyber hygiene, awareness programs, workshops, and student participation (reported 13 Jul 2026). **[FACT]**

**Deprioritized for now:** coding communities and LinkedIn (use for interviews only, because of sampling bias), paid ads, app-store listings, content marketing. One channel is enough for a first test.

## 9. Reverse SWOT Analysis

A reverse SWOT challenges each item. Strengths are tested as possible weaknesses, and weaknesses as possible leverage.

| Standard item | Reverse view | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength:** you can build and iterate alone | Building is not the bottleneck; demand and trust are. Building more is a way to avoid interviews. | Cap build time; interviews first |
| **Strength:** you are in the target group | Your habits are one data point and may bias your design | Interview people unlike you |
| **Weakness:** no security credibility | Honest transparency can be a trust asset: publish sources and limits | Public method; expert review |
| **Weakness:** no startup experience | Low burn and no sunk pride make quick stops cheap | Set kill criteria now |
| **Opportunity:** India's data-protection rules | A regulation is not a customer; companies, not students, carry most obligations | Treat it as a trigger, not demand |
| **Opportunity:** UGC and I4C awareness activity | Awareness activity may be unpaid and handled by free resources | Ask what they pay for |
| **Threat:** free alternatives | Free tools may already be good enough; their existence does not prove a gap | Ask users what they have tried and what still hurts |
| **Threat:** platforms add cross-service views | Platforms may have little incentive to compare themselves with rivals **[ASSUMPTION]** | Keep the cross-company view as the test of a real gap |

## Sustainability Verdict

On current evidence the business is **not yet sustainable**: it has no paying customer, consumer revenue at realistic reach is small, and the institutional route has a verified awareness hook but an unproven budget. Whether it can become sustainable depends on three things to validate next: that students act on a sourced plan, that at least one college will run a paid or formal pilot, and that you can produce trustworthy, maintained data. Run the 30-day test before building anything more.
